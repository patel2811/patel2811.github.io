Understanding Uber Riders

Using primary research and statistical analysis to understand brand perception, rider experience, loyalty, and customer segments.

Graduate Market Research Project
MKTG 610 · 2025

The Research Question

What factors influence Uber users' brand perception, satisfaction, loyalty, willingness to recommend, and willingness to pay?

The research examined brand perception, customer loyalty, marketing effectiveness, user experience, accessibility, and feedback mechanisms using survey data from Uber riders.

Project Snapshot

Research Method

Qualtrics survey + SPSS analysis

Survey Period

September 17 – October 4, 2025

Sample

73 reported survey responses

Analytical Approach

The project combined primary survey research with statistical testing and customer segmentation to connect rider experience with broader brand outcomes.

01 · Design the Research

A structured Qualtrics questionnaire captured brand perceptions, recommendation intent, satisfaction, ride behavior, app experience, promotions, safety, demographics, and other control variables.

02 · Compare Experiences

Paired-samples t-tests were used to examine differences between overall brand perception and recent ride experience, as well as recommendation intent.

03 · Identify Drivers

Regression analysis was used to evaluate variables associated with brand perception.

04 · Segment Customers

Cluster analysis identified distinct rider groups based on perception, usage, demographics, tenure, and satisfaction.

05 · Translate Findings Into Action

Statistical findings were translated into recommendations around loyalty, brand communication, customer experience, and feedback mechanisms.

ANALYSIS 01

The Brand Experience Gap

The first paired-samples t-test compared respondents' overall perception of Uber with their rating of their most recent ride.

Key Finding

The submitted analysis reported a statistically significant difference between overall brand perception and recent ride experience. The project used this gap to explore whether customers' immediate service experiences and broader brand perceptions were fully aligned.

ANALYSIS 02

What Shapes Brand Perception?

A multiple regression model was used to examine which variables were associated with users' overall perception of the Uber brand.

Model Result

The submitted model reported an adjusted R² of 0.589. Significant predictors in the analysis included likelihood to recommend Uber, willingness to pay more for greater quality or convenience, age, and device used to request Uber.

ANALYSIS 03

Five Distinct Rider Segments

Cluster analysis identified five rider groups with different usage patterns, tenure, satisfaction levels, demographics, and brand perceptions.

Legendary Loyalists

Older, long-tenured riders with high satisfaction and very positive brand perceptions.

Moving Millennials

Younger riders with moderate usage, high satisfaction, and room to deepen brand connection.

Fickle Feelers

Longer-term riders with lower satisfaction and weaker brand sentiment.

Casual Cruisers

Moderate-age, infrequent users with more neutral brand perceptions.

Uberfanatics

The largest segment, characterized by frequent usage, long tenure, and high satisfaction.

ANALYSIS 04

From Research to Marketing Action

The findings were translated into strategies for strengthening brand connection, loyalty, personalization, user experience, and feedback.

Brand Connection

Connect the service experience with Uber's broader brand values through in-app and post-ride communications.

Loyalty & Retention

Explore referral, reward, and status-based programs designed around usage and tenure.

Personalization

Use customer characteristics and behavioral insights to create more relevant engagement.

Experience & Accessibility

Investigate app performance and device-specific experience differences before scaling improvements.

Additional Research Insights

Recommendation Intent

The project found a significant difference between overall brand perception and likelihood to recommend Uber, despite a strong relationship between the two measures.

App Experience

Statistical testing identified differences associated with device type for selected app-experience questions, although the sample was too small for more detailed post-hoc analysis.

Income & Ride Type

The project did not find a significant relationship between household income and the type of Uber ride selected.

Research Limitations

The study used a convenience sample and self-reported responses. The submitted materials also note limitations related to sample size and the ability to conduct more detailed subgroup testing. Findings should therefore be interpreted as directional rather than representative of all Uber riders.
