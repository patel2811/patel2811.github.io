PROJECT 01 · DATA VISUALIZATION

Tableau Workforce Analytics Data Storytelling

California Employment & Wage Trends

A 2020–2025 analysis of employment demand, wage growth, occupation trends, and geographic differences across California.

Dataset OEWS
Period 2020–2025
Scope 22 occupation groups
Tool Tableau

01 / THE QUESTION

Do employment growth and wage growth move together?

The project examined employment and wage trends across California occupation groups to investigate whether high-growth occupations experienced corresponding wage increases between 2020 and 2025.

The analysis used Occupational Employment and Wage Statistics data to compare occupation-level and geographic patterns.

02 / FEATURED OUTPUT

Employment & Wage Analysis Dashboard

The final Tableau dashboard brings together occupational, geographic, employment, and wage analyses into a single view.

03 / APPROACH

Building comparable measures

The analysis established a 2020 baseline and then normalized employment and wage changes so occupation groups could be compared on a common scale.

01 · Establish a baseline

Created fixed 2020 employment and wage values for each occupation group.

02 · Normalize the change

Converted differences from the 2020 baseline into proportional changes.

03 · Compare occupation groups

Examined employment and wage movement across selected occupation groups.

04 · Add geographic context

Used geographic visualization to compare California locations and regional patterns.

04 / ANALYSIS 01

Employment & Wage Trends

A dual-axis Tableau visualization compared employment and wage trends over the 2020–2025 period.

What to notice

Employment and wage movements were not uniform across occupations. The analysis showed periods of volatility and different patterns of change across occupation groups.

05 / ANALYSIS 02

Normalized Wage vs. Employment Growth

Normalized employment and wage changes were compared across selected STEM and healthcare occupation groups.

Why normalization mattered

Occupation groups differ substantially in their absolute employment and wage levels. Normalization made it possible to compare the direction and magnitude of change rather than raw values alone.

06 / ANALYSIS 03

California Geographic Analysis

A geographic Tableau visualization was used to examine regional differences in employment and wage patterns across California.

Geographic perspective

The map added geographic context to the occupation-level analysis and made regional differences easier to compare visually.

07 / KEY FINDINGS

What the data showed

Employment ≠ wages

Employment and wage growth did not consistently move together across occupation groups.

Patterns varied over time

The project observed substantial movement during the earlier part of the period, with several occupations showing more consistent patterns later in the timeframe.

Healthcare Support stood out

Among the selected STEM and healthcare groups, Healthcare Support showed increases in both normalized employment and wages.

Wage growth can occur alongside decline

Some occupation groups experienced wage growth even when employment declined, challenging a simple one-to-one relationship between demand and compensation.

08 / BUSINESS IMPLICATIONS

From analysis to action

Recruitment & Retention

Focus attention on occupations where employment and wages are both increasing.

Upskilling & Role Design

Consider targeted upskilling or role redesign where wage growth occurs alongside declining employment.

Workforce Planning

Use employment and wage trends together when evaluating workforce needs and compensation decisions.
